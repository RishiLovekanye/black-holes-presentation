    Two ways to explain gravity.

    Newton (1687): gravity is a force. It pulls masses together. It acts at once across space.

    Einstein (1915): gravity is not a force. Mass bends spacetime. Objects follow the bent space.

    1687Newton
    1915General relativity
    43″Mercury, per century
    Same orbit · two explanations
    RishikeshSay: Newton explains most orbits. But Newton cannot explain a small shift in the orbit of Mercury: 43 arcseconds each century. Einstein's theory gives the correct number. In 1919 Eddington measured starlight that bends near the Sun. This supported Einstein.

    Black hole, horizon and Schwarzschild radius.

    A black hole is a region where gravity is so strong that nothing can leave. Not even light.

    The event horizon is the boundary beyond which future-directed light cannot escape to a distant observer. Karl Schwarzschild found the solution in 1916.

    Key values rs = 2GM/c² · G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻² · M = mass (kg) · c = 2.998×10⁸ m/s · rs in metres.
    rph = 1.5 rs · photon sphere of a non-rotating hole. Light can orbit here, but the orbit is unstable. It is outside the horizon.
    ve = √(2GM/r) · classical escape speed (m/s). The formula gives c at r = rs, but the horizon is not a surface where objects fail to reach escape speed. In general relativity, inside rs every future path, light too, leads inward.
    K = 12/rs⁴ · curvature (Kretschmann scalar) at the horizon, in m⁻⁴. Bigger holes have smaller K.

    RIRishikesh Sonar · Formation · 1 of 4

    Gravitational collapse.

    A star shines because fusion pushes outward. Gravity pulls inward. The two forces balance.

    When the fuel ends, the balance ends. The core falls inward. If the core has more than about 2 to 3 solar masses, no known force can stop it. A black hole forms.

    Life of a massive star · click a stage
    RishikeshSay: Chandrasekhar showed that a white dwarf cannot be heavier than about 1.4 solar masses. A neutron star has a similar limit, near 2 to 3 solar masses. Above that limit, collapse continues.

    RIRishikesh Sonar · Formation · 2 of 4

    The Oppenheimer–Snyder model (1939).

    J. Robert Oppenheimer and Hartland Snyder studied a round cloud of dust. It has no pressure and no spin. It collapses.

    A far observer sees the cloud slow down and fade near the horizon. A falling observer crosses the horizon and reaches the center in a finite time.

    Two views of one collapse · schematic
    RishikeshSay: This was the first full calculation of black-hole formation in general relativity. The model is simple, and that is the problem. Real stars are not perfect.

    RIRishikesh Sonar · Formation · 3 of 4

    Early scientific doubts.

    Many scientists did not accept the result. Some said nature must stop the collapse. Some said real stars are not perfect.

    Click each date to see the doubt and the answer.

    RishikeshSay: In 1935 Eddington rejected the idea. He believed that a law of nature must prevent it. Einstein had doubts in 1939. In 1963 two Soviet physicists argued that a real collapse could avoid a singularity. All of these doubts depended on the same question: does the result need a perfect shape?

    RIRishikesh Sonar · Formation · 4 of 4

    Why would a star bounce back?

    Three reasons were given. Pressure could rise and push back. Spin could throw matter outward. Lumps could make matter miss the center.

    The 1939 model had none of these. Nobody knew if a real star would still collapse. The answer needed a proof that does not depend on a perfect shape.

    Perfect sphere or real star?
    RishikeshSay: The picture shows the question. On the left, a perfect shell. On the right, a lumpy shell. Does the lumpy shell also collapse to a point? Next: how Roger Penrose answered in 1965.

    RIRishikesh Sonar · Penrose · 1 of 4

    Penrose's 1965 discovery.

    Roger Penrose did not solve a perfect star. He used global geometry and topology. These study the shape of the whole spacetime, not one small part.

    He asked: where can all light rays go? This is the causal structure. The light cones show it. Near a black hole, they tilt inward.

    Outgoing lightIngoing lightFalling observer
    Light cones · Eddington–Finkelstein diagram
    RishikeshSay: Time goes up. Distance goes right. At the horizon, the outer edge of the cone points straight up. Inside the horizon, every path into the future goes to the center. The center is not a place. It is a time in the future.

    RIRishikesh Sonar · Penrose · 2 of 4

    Trapped surfaces.

    A trapped surface is a closed surface in space. Light that leaves it outward still gets pulled inward. Light that leaves it inward also converges.

    The area of the light front gets smaller in both directions. Gravity is too strong for any light to move outward.

    A flash of light from a sphere
    RishikeshSay: Left, normal space: the outgoing light front grows. Middle, the horizon: the outgoing front stays the same size. Right, a trapped surface: both fronts shrink. Once such a surface forms, it cannot be undone.

    RIRishikesh Sonar · Penrose · 3 of 4

    The singularity theorem.

    Penrose, 1965Assume:
    1. General relativity is true.
    2. Gravity is always attractive.
    3. Spacetime has a non-compact Cauchy surface.
    4. A closed trapped surface exists.
    Then spacetime is geodesically incomplete: at least one light ray has an end. This is a singularity.

    The proof is by contradiction. No symmetry is needed. The Penrose diagram shows it: light from before the horizon escapes. Light from after it reaches the singularity.

    Penrose diagram of a collapsing star
    RishikeshSay: The paper has three pages. It uses topology instead of a perfect shape. The result is general. A real, lumpy star also makes a singularity, if it makes a trapped surface. Stephen Hawking and Penrose later used the same idea for the whole universe.

    RIRishikesh Sonar · Penrose · 4 of 4

    The 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics.

    On 6 October 2020, the prize went to three scientists. Roger Penrose won half. He showed that black-hole formation is a robust prediction of general relativity.

    Reinhard Genzel and Andrea Ghez shared the other half. They found a supermassive compact object at the center of our galaxy.

    TheoryPenrose
    EvidenceGenzel and Ghez
    Star S2 around Sgr A* · 1 s ≈ 1 year
    RishikeshSay: The prize joins theory and observation. Penrose proved that black holes must form. Genzel and Ghez tracked stars, such as S2, around an invisible mass of 4 million suns. Next: the new observations.

    RIRishikesh Sonar · Applications · 1 of 8

    A telescope as big as Earth.

    The Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) links radio telescopes around the world. The method is very long baseline interferometry. The telescopes work as one.

    The distance between two stations sets the sharpness. The Earth is the dish. The EHT can see details of about 20 microarcseconds.

    RishikeshSay: Each pair of telescopes makes one baseline. More telescopes give more baselines and a sharper image. The data from each site is stored on disks and later combined in a computer.

    The first images of black holes.

    April 2019: the EHT showed M87*. It is 55 million light-years away. Mass: 6.5 billion Suns.

    May 2022: the EHT showed Sagittarius A*, at the center of our galaxy. Mass: 4.3 million Suns.

    Each image shows a bright ring around a dark shadow. The ring is light that gravity bends. Press EHT view to see how the telescope sees it.

    Gravitational waves and LIGO.

    Two black holes orbit each other. They lose energy as gravitational waves. They move closer and merge.

    On 14 September 2015, LIGO detected such a merger (GW150914). The black holes had 36 and 29 Suns. About 3 Suns of mass became waves. The arms of LIGO changed by about 10⁻¹⁸ m, hundreds of times less than a proton.

    RIRishikesh Sonar · Impact · 4 of 8

    Black holes shape galaxies.

    Almost every large galaxy has a supermassive black hole at its center. Gas that falls in makes the black hole very bright. This is an active galactic nucleus (AGN).

    Jets and winds from the AGN heat the gas. Hot gas cannot form new stars. So the black hole helps to control how the galaxy grows. Move the slider.

    A spiral galaxy · 3D · drag to turn
    AGN activity
    RishikeshSay: The black hole is very small compared with the galaxy. But the energy it releases can stop star formation across the whole galaxy. This is called AGN feedback. The black-hole mass and the galaxy bulge are linked.

    RIRishikesh Sonar · Open problem · 5 of 8

    The information paradox.

    In 1974 Stephen Hawking showed that black holes emit thermal radiation. They slowly lose mass and then vanish.

    The radiation seems to carry no information about what fell in. But quantum physics says information cannot be destroyed. This is the paradox.

    In 1993 Don Page proposed that the information in the radiation first rises and then falls. Calculations in 2019 support this. The problem is not fully solved.

    Hawking radiation and the Page curve
    RishikeshSay: A pair of particles forms at the horizon. One falls in. One escapes. The black hole loses mass. The curve on the right shows how much information the radiation holds. Hawking's curve keeps rising. The Page curve falls at the end. This would keep the information.

    RIRishikesh Sonar · Open problem · 6 of 8

    Why we need quantum gravity.

    General relativity predicts a singularity. There, the curvature is infinite. A theory that gives infinite values has reached its limit.

    At the Planck length (1.6 × 10⁻³⁵ m), quantum effects must matter. We do not have a tested theory of quantum gravity. Two candidates are string theory and loop quantum gravity.

    Zoom into spacetime · 3D
    Zoom
    RishikeshSay: At normal scales spacetime looks smooth. As we zoom to the Planck length, we expect it to be rough and to change quickly. This picture is an idea, not a measurement. Nobody has seen this scale.

    RIRishikesh Sonar · Conclusion · 8 of 8

    A prediction on paper became a part of the sky.

    Newton gave us gravity as a force. Einstein gave us curved spacetime. Oppenheimer and Snyder showed that collapse can make a black hole. Penrose proved that it must. EHT and LIGO now observe them. Singularities and the information paradox show where new physics must start.
